## 🗓 Narrative Context
 
### Summary
The crew has successfully infiltrated Nexus Station under the guise of high-stakes gamblers and tech consultants. After three days of reconnaissance, they've mapped security patrols, identified the datacore's location, and established contact with an inside sympathizer—a janitor named Kip who has access to maintenance corridors.
 
The target: a quantum datacore containing evidence of corporate fraud by Helion Industries. The client wants it intact and undetected. The problem: the datacore is in a vault with biometric locks, rotating guard shifts, and AI surveillance. The crew has 36 hours before their cover identities expire.
 
Rax wants to go loud if things go wrong. Zephyr insists on a clean, undetected extraction. Byte has hacked partial access to security feeds but needs physical proximity to disable the vault's AI. The team is ready—but the plan has too many variables.
 
### Notable Moments
- Zephyr won 40,000 credits at the casino, raising their profile (helpful for access, risky for attention)
- Byte discovered the AI monitoring system has a blind spot during shift changes (18-minute window)
- Rax befriended a security guard named Torres, who mentioned increased alert levels due to "corporate espionage concerns"
- Kip the janitor revealed there's a secondary access point through the air filtration system—but it's a tight squeeze
 
### Unresolved Threads
- Is the increased security related to the crew's job, or something else?
- Can Kip be trusted? (He's asking for a 15% cut now)
- What's on the other restricted datacores in the vault?
- Torres might suspect something—or he might just be friendly
- Zephyr's gambling winnings have attracted attention from station management
